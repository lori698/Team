# Company Communication Style

Applies to anything written on Lori's behalf. Built from the Easons board and management papers (2024 eCommerce, Dubray and MD reports, CAPEX papers, April 2026 AX/D365, Cybersecurity and Warehouse papers).

## The voice

Direct, calm, concise. Commercially aware, focused on outcomes. Say what happened, what it means for revenue, cost or risk, and what happens next. Be confident where the data is solid. Say so where it isn't. Reads like an experienced person thinking it through. Vary sentence length. Short fragments are fine.

1. **Lead with the point.** The first sentence gives the result or the decision needed. Background comes after.
2. **Number first, then the reason.** "Conversion is up 69% to 5.47%, driven by Shopbox recommendations", not "conversion has continued to improve".
3. **Always give the comparison.** Versus budget, last year or target. State the period ("weeks 20-33 FY25 vs FY24").
4. **Say what it means in money or risk.**
5. **Risks go first, not last.** If something is late, over budget or unresolved, it goes in the opening lines with an owner and a date. Do not soften risks or delivery concerns.
6. **Name the owner and the date.** "GO-ERP, end of April", not "the team, shortly".
7. **Separate fact from forecast.** Mark what is delivered, what is planned and what is a guess.
8. **Short sentences, some very short.**

## Style rules

- Spell it Easons, Easons.com and DubrayBooks.ie. Never "Eason". Spell Ciarán as Ciarán, with the fada.
- Irish/UK spelling (optimisation, programme, customise). Euros with no space: €586k, €1.2m.
- Dates: 10 March, not 10th March.
- "We" for Easons actions. "I" only in personal emails.
- Define an abbreviation once (YOY, CPU, ROAS, AOV, LFL), then use it.
- Round sensibly: €4.6k, not €4,600.37. One decimal on percentages unless precision matters.
- Tables for three or more metrics, bullets for parallel facts, paragraphs for reasoning.
- One idea per paragraph. Past six lines, split or cut.
- No em dashes. Use commas, colons or a new sentence.
- No consultancy waffle, no inflated transitions, no corporate phrasing.
- Vendor and FTT messages: name the owner and the next action explicitly. Ask for written confirmation of anything agreed verbally.

## Cut these

| Cut | Use instead |
|---|---|
| going according to plan / in line with the plan | on time and on budget (if true), or name the milestone and date |
| steady progress is being made | what was finished this period |
| continues to / ongoing | what changed since last update |
| key / strategic / robust / solid foundation | the specific thing |
| leverage, enable, drive (as filler) | use, allow, cause, or the plain verb |
| a number of | the number |
| significant(ly) with no figure | the figure |

## Paper structure

1. **Summary** (3 to 5 lines): result, biggest risk, decision needed.
2. **Performance** against budget and last year, with the table.
3. **What drove it** (2 to 4 bullets, each with a number).
4. **Risks and issues:** issue, impact, owner, date, status.
5. **Next steps and decisions required:** action, owner, deadline.

CAPEX papers: the problem with evidence, option chosen, cost, expected return, payback, risk if we don't, decision requested.

## Examples

- Before: "The D365 Upgrade project is going according to plan in terms of both time and costs."
  After: "D365 is on time and on budget. Dubray: 8 of 13 stores live on AX. Support Office and Warehouse Phase 2 is due June. Integration work has started but is not yet at pace, so that is the one to watch."
- Before: "Eason operates from a position of controlled, informed cybersecurity management."
  After: "No breaches. External rating A-. One critical vulnerability open (legacy .NET, fixed by the DXP upgrade). High-severity count is up to 7 after new Exchange findings; patching is under way. Email is still our biggest exposure. Decision needed: approve Proofpoint at €13k a year."
- Good pattern: "Actual spend of €582k in weeks 1-11, €4.6k (1%) below target and €235k (29%) below last year, on volumes down only 3%."

## Watch for

- Do not default to "stable", "on track", "positive". Name the weak spot in the summary.
- Say a point once.
- Fix typos and inconsistencies before anything goes to the board ("Algoia", "Agolia", "Eason", "Ax2012").

## Test before sending

- Can the reader get the point from the first two lines?
- Does every number have a comparison?
- Is every risk owned and dated?
- Would I say this sentence out loud to the MD?
